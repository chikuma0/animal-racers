# Hero-art-7 — rejected Lion study

This experiment does **not** meet the cinematic character target and must not replace the canonical Lion. Parent review rejected the helmet/curtain construction and then the broad ruff study. The last change is limited to removing the upright collar/clasp intrusion into the muzzle. Full motion rendering is deliberately withheld after this visual rejection; numerical clip sampling is not a substitute.

## What improved and can inform a reconstruction

The face uses one connected surface around real almond orbital apertures, with cheeks and nasal bridge in that same surface. Its shallow eye inserts sit behind shaped rims instead of protruding spheres. The source skull has one connected component and two intended eye boundary loops. Its mandible remains independently articulated. Those choices improve the old round eyes and separate spherical whisker pads, although the current expression, shallow eyelids and jaw fit still need artistic work.

The same authored head coordinates transfer through the existing head/jaw rest matrices into race and upright form. Original body mesh, weights, corrective shapes and action curves are unchanged. Exported bone and race corrective-weight comparisons cover 41 samples of every clip. These checks establish structural preservation only.

## Why the mane remains unsuccessful

The rejected first versions placed many flattened cubic tubes over a smooth backing. Making those tubes longer produced curtain hair; fluting a taller backing produced a helmet. These were changes to surface decoration without a convincing underlying Lion mane volume.

The current broad ruff is a double-walled radial annulus, 13 radial rows by 92 angular columns, around the face. It is connected as a mesh, but its construction still behaves like an inflated collar. Its inner/outer loops do not describe the irregular transitions from temple to cheek, beneath the jaw, behind the ear and onto the shoulder. Rounding the rear removes the flat plate defect; it does not make those anatomical transitions.

Eighteen individually placed large swept ruff curves and smaller accents improve the silhouette, but each uses the same radius-over-length profile. Their thick roots, smooth cross-sections and taper therefore still read as repeated inflated locks. They intersect the backing rather than sharing its topology. Adding more strips, grooves or a fur color texture will not remove that repeated volume or repair its roots. The rigid head weighting also makes the entire ruff follow one bone rather than having a neck attachment and secondary tip motion.

## Required reconstruction, before another detail pass

1. Establish a real three-dimensional skull/neck/ruff blockout against front, side and three-quarter concept proportions. Keep the successful eye aperture and continuous nasal-bridge ideas, but rebuild cheekbone, lower cheek, jaw hinge and orbital depth as connected volumes.
2. Sculpt the mane as a small number of interlocking, asymmetric masses with different cross-sections and lengths. Roots must emerge continuously around ears and cheeks; the inner burnt-sienna ruff must taper onto the chest. The current regular annulus and identical tube profile should not be the starting surface.
3. Retopologize that sculpt with deliberate loops and flow. Use enough geometry for silhouette and roots; bake smaller fur flow and grooves from an authored high-resolution surface into normal/color maps instead of stacking more separate tubes. Check underside, rear and maximum jaw opening before further detail.
4. Fit the collar to the reconstructed neck/chest, and weight the lower ruff to the neck with the upper mass following the head. Any extra mane joints or corrective shapes need an explicit animation/export contract review; do not silently change the currently preserved rig.
5. Require matched concept-scale views first. Only a visibly better blockout should proceed to full affected cycles, runtime lighting/contact review and owner acceptance.

A credibly licensed lion mesh may be useful as an anatomical base if its source topology, provenance and editability can be inspected. It would still need species styling, facial work, retopology, two-form fitting, deliberate weights and the existing timing/contact checks. A license or a high polygon count is not art acceptance.

## Scope of the final accessory correction

The smaller upright face exposed the previous neck-weighted clasp at jaw height (clasp rest bounds 1.781–1.914 m; collar 1.799–1.935 m). In this isolated study only, the clasp/fold are lowered onto the chest, the collar is fitted around that surface, and these costume pieces follow the existing chest bone. Body and rig remain unchanged. Race costume placement is unchanged. No production files are promoted.
